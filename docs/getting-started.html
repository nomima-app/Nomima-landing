Getting started

From download to your first connected note. Nomima runs entirely on your Mac — no account, no cloud, no setup wizard.

Install

  1. Request your download link from the Nomima site — enter your email and we'll send a link to the .dmg. Nomima is free and built for Apple Silicon Macs (M1 and later, macOS 11+).
  2. Open the .dmg and drag Nomima into your Applications folder.
  3. Launch it. That’s the whole setup — there is no sign-up, login, or onboarding form.

Where your notes live: on first launch Nomima creates a local vault on your Mac. Everything — notes, files, the knowledge graph — is stored there. It leaves your device only when you choose to use Summon (AI) or publish a note. See Settings & privacy for details.

The Welcome notebook

A fresh vault opens with a “Welcome to Nomima” notebook. It’s a live playground, not a read-only tour: it already contains real @mentions, #hashtags, tasks and dates, so the Knowledge Graph and the colored minimap on the right edge light up immediately. Edit it, break it, delete it — it’s yours.

The interface at a glance

The whole window is just three things: your notebooks on the left, the editor in the middle, and everything Nomima builds from your writing around the edges — a table of contents, a colored minimap, a live graph, and the AI assistant.

Also worth knowing: Home is your daily agenda (overdue, today, upcoming), and a Smart View reorganizes the whole vault by mention, hashtag, date, task or file — both built automatically from what you write.

Where notifications live: Nomima has no separate notifications bell. Anything due or overdue surfaces in two places you already use — the overdue and upcoming badges on Home, and the Alerts lens in Smart View (its pill carries a red count when something needs attention).

Your first ten minutes

Do these in the Welcome notebook (or a new one). Each step takes seconds — by the end you’ll have used the entire core loop.

  1. Create a notebook

    Click the new-notebook button in the sidebar, type a title, and press Enter — the caret drops straight into the page. Then just write; plain paragraphs are fine. Structure is opt-in, never required.

  2. Add a task list

    Type [] then a space at the start of a line, or type /task and pick Task List. Give it a title, press Enter, and add items — check them off from anywhere and every view stays in sync.

  3. Tag a person with @

    Type @ and a name — @Sara — and press Tab to accept. It becomes a @Sara pill connected to everything else that mentions Sara. As you type, Nomima even suggests names you’ve used before.

  4. Tag a project with #

    Type # and a topic — #launch → #launch. Mentions are the who/what; hashtags are the themes and projects. Both become filters and graph connections.

  5. Write a date in plain words

    Type “call the venue tomorrow 3pm” — the words turn into a live tomorrow 3pm pill that shows up in Home and the Dates view. Turn any date into a countdown reminder with a desktop alert.

  6. Watch it connect

    Glance at the cover graph above the note: @Sara and #launch are already nodes, wired to this note and to anywhere else you’ve used them. You built a knowledge graph without thinking about it.

  7. Find anything with ⌘K

    Press ⌘K to search notebooks, blocks, every mention, hashtag, date and file — and to run commands. Press Enter on a tag to open its Smart View lens; stack several to filter by all or any.

✓

That’s the whole core loop

Write naturally, tag as you go, and Nomima organizes everything for you — no databases to configure, and no filing required (your tags do the organizing). Folders are there when you want to group notebooks by hand, never a chore you have to keep up. Everything below is just going deeper.

Organize the sidebar

Your notebooks live in the sidebar, and you can arrange them however you like:

  • Reorder by hand. Drag a notebook up or down — a glowing line shows exactly where it will land, and the order sticks. (Pinned notebooks still sit at the top.)
  • Nest a page under another. Drag a notebook onto the middle of another one to tuck it inside as a child page; the parent gets an expand arrow. Drag it back out to un-nest.
  • Group with folders. Click the folder button next to the notebook list to make a folder, then drag notebooks onto it to bundle them — even notebooks that already have child pages. A folder's ⋯ menu lets you rename it, give it an icon, add a notebook, or delete it. Deleting a folder keeps your notebooks — they just move back out to the top level.
  • Open a folder's overview. Click a folder's name (not its icon — that still expands it inline) to open a full-page overview of everything inside it. Toggle between a List that keeps the structure — notebooks with their pages indented underneath — and a Cards grid that lays every notebook and buried page out as even, preview-rich tiles, so a page tucked several levels down is just as easy to spot as a top-level one; your choice is remembered. A search box at the top filters as you type and looks inside your notes, not just titles.

The drag tells you what it'll do: a line means "reorder here," a highlighted row means "drop inside," and a red ⊘ means "you can't drop that there" (for example, a folder can't go inside another folder). Aim anywhere at a folder — its header, one of its notebooks, or the space between them — or at a page that has subpages, and the whole group lights up, everything nested inside it included, with deeper levels in a deeper shade: that's the group you're about to join, shown before you let go.

Find anything

Press ⌘K to open the command palette. It searches notebooks, blocks, every mention and hashtag, dates (“tomorrow”, “friday”…), and files — and it runs commands like creating a notebook or opening Settings. Press Enter on a tag to open its Smart View lens; you can even stack several tags to filter by all or any of them.

Retrace your steps: Nomima remembers where you’ve been. Use the back and forward arrows in the titlebar — or ⌘[ and ⌘] — to move through the notebooks and views you’ve visited, and the breadcrumbs next to them show where you are.
