Summon

An assistant that actually knows your notes. Summon searches your vault, answers questions, creates notebooks and streams writing straight into your editor — with whatever AI model you chose.

Before you start: Summon is free — it just needs at least one configured AI model with your own API key for Anthropic, OpenAI or Google. Summon connects only to each provider’s current frontier models.

Opening Summon

  • Click the glowing button in the bottom-right corner — it breathes quietly when idle and animates while Summon is working, with a badge counting in-flight tasks.
  • Or type /summon in any note — your current block and selection travel along as context.
  • Summon opens as a compact dock on the right edge; the expand button promotes it to a full-screen view. Close with Esc — the conversation survives, even as you switch notebooks.

Chat or Note — your choice, per message

Every response has a toggle deciding where it lands:

  • Chat — stays in the conversation, ephemeral.
  • Note — streams live into your editor, token by token, as a new notebook or appended to the current one. You watch the note write itself, as real blocks — headings, lists, checklists — not a pasted wall of text.

Choosing a mode

The mode selector sits at the top of the Summon panel. Pick one before you send:

ModeWhat it does
AutoSummon decides — it picks the depth appropriate for your question. Good default for most prompts.
QuickFewer tool rounds, faster answer. Best for simple look-ups (“what’s overdue?”, “when did I last mention @Sara?”).
Deep ResearchFull multi-pass vault sweep before writing. Summon reads broadly, synthesises and cites passages. Use for “write a brief on everything I know about X” or “audit all my notes on the rebrand”.
CreateFocuses Summon on drafting and editing rather than researching. Use when you already know what you want and just need it written.

Composer Smart Tags

Type @ or # directly inside the Summon input to attach a Smart Tag to your prompt. The pill appears inline — and Summon automatically receives the entity’s full profile (attributes, linked notes) or the top matching vault passages as injected context. You can coin a brand-new tag on the spot if it doesn’t exist yet. This is the fastest way to scope a question: ”Summarise everything I know about @Priya and #launch” — no need to mention them by name in plain text.

Asking about your vault

  • Vault Q&A — ask “what do I know about X?” and Summon searches your notes from multiple angles, including by meaning (semantic retrieval), and synthesises the answer right in the conversation — without creating a notebook you didn’t ask for.
  • Scope cards — for open-ended research questions, Summon first shows a short scope card so you can pick the angle (or multi-select a few). It then researches exactly what you meant.
  • Grounding badge — answers drawn from your own notes carry a small From your vault badge (or a passage count), so you can tell vault-sourced answers apart from the model’s general knowledge.
  • RSS Memories are searchable too — articles you’ve pinned as RSS Memories (title, source and full text) are indexed when you save them, so Summon can pull from them as well.

Writing & auditing in place

  • Conversational edits in place — ask Summon to “expand on the decision loop” or “go deeper on the intro” and it inserts the new content after the relevant section, not at the bottom of the note.
  • Gap audit — ask “what’s missing here?” or “is this comprehensive?” and Summon audits the open notebook and lists specific gaps as one-click chips you can fill immediately.

Memory, steps & nudges

  • Persistent memory (Brain button) — a memory panel behind the Brain button in the chat input shows everything Summon has learned about you across sessions. Add facts manually, or delete any it got wrong.
  • Live agent steps — when Summon uses its tools, the steps stream live as collapsible “thinking” steps so you can follow its reasoning.
  • Proactive nudges — Summon can surface cold notes you might want to revisit.

Agent permissions

Summon’s reach beyond your vault is off by default and controlled in Settings → Summon Agent:

PermissionWhat it unlocks
Web searchSummon can search online (via DuckDuckGo or your configured Brave Search key) to supplement vault answers with current information.
Filesystem accessRead files in your Documents, Desktop and Downloads folders — useful for "import this report" or "summarise the PDF I just downloaded".
Shell commandsStrictly allowlist-based: you add the exact commands it may run; chaining and piping are blocked so it can’t do anything you haven’t approved.
Calendar & RemindersRead upcoming events and reminders from macOS Calendar and Reminders apps — "what’s on my calendar this week?"
ContactsLook up people in macOS Contacts — "get me James’s email address".
MailSearch and read recent messages from macOS Mail — "did anyone email me about the invoice?"
SpotlightSearch your whole Mac for files and folders by name or content — "find the latest version of the spec doc".

Privacy reminder: when Summon answers a question about your notes, the relevant note content is sent to the AI provider you configured — that’s how the model reads it. Mac system tools (Calendar, Contacts, Mail) only send the data relevant to your specific request. Nothing is ever sent anywhere when you’re not actively using Summon.
