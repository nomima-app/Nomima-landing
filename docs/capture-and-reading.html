Capture & reading

Bring the outside world into your vault — files, web pages, videos — and read or watch it right inside Nomima, without a browser in the way.

Everything you drag, paste or drop into a note can do one of two things. It can become part of your knowledge — its text extracted, searchable, and woven into your Knowledge Graph so the people, projects and ideas inside it connect to the rest of your vault. Or it can simply live as an attachment: there when you need it, but out of the way of your thinking. You decide per item with a small brain toggle, and you can change your mind at any time.

Files as knowledge

Drop a file straight onto a note, or use the /files block and its folder dropzone to pull in a whole folder at once. Nomima copies each file into your vault, then reads the text inside it — PDFs, documents, slides, spreadsheets, and more.

By default, that extracted text is folded into your knowledge: the file shows up in full-text search, its entities light up in the Knowledge Graph, and it connects to related notes automatically. If you'd rather a file just sit there as a plain attachment, flip its brain toggle off — it stays fully usable, but its contents stop feeding search and the graph. Toggle it back on whenever you want it to rejoin the conversation.

  • Attached vs. part of knowledge — every file carries this state on its pill, so you always know at a glance whether it's contributing to your vault or simply parked there.
  • Open it in place — PDFs open in a clean in-app viewer, so you can read without launching another app. Any file can also be opened in its default app in one click.

Tip: the brain toggle is per file, so you can keep the signed contract as a quiet attachment while letting the research report you dropped next to it enrich your whole vault.

Bookmark a web page

Paste a link into a note and it turns into a bookmark card right away — you don't wait around. The card appears instantly with the URL, and Nomima fetches the page in the background, filling in the title, description and details as they arrive.

While it's at it, Nomima pulls the page's readable article text and, by default, folds it into your knowledge — same brain toggle as files, so you can opt any bookmark out and keep it as a plain link. This is where Nomima shines for research: the actual words of every page you save sit together in one searchable place, connected to everything else you're thinking about.

Read it here

Any bookmark whose article Nomima could parse gets a Read button. Click it and the page opens in a clean, distraction-free reader right inside the app — real structure preserved (headings, links, lists, quotes), none of the ads, popups or clutter of the open web. It's private, too: images are stripped, so nothing phones home while you read. If a page can't be parsed into an article, the reader falls back gracefully rather than showing you a broken view.

Some pages you want to see live and whole — a dashboard, an interactive tool, a site that refuses to be simplified. That's what Pop out is for: it opens the real page in a floating web-view window that works for any site, even ones that normally refuse to be embedded. Keep it on top beside your notes while you write.

Watch videos inline

Paste a YouTube link and it plays inside Nomima, not off in your browser. Nomima remembers where you stopped, so a progress bar sits under each video with Resume and Start over — pick up exactly where you left off, days later.

The best part is that the video follows you. Keep it playing while you scroll down the note or move to another view, and it tucks itself into a small picture-in-picture corner player so you never lose your place. Leave the app entirely and it becomes a native floating mini-player that stays on top of your other apps — with play/pause, volume, and a Back to the note button to jump straight home. A paused video politely stays put and won't pop out.

Your privacy stays intact. Text extraction and reading all happen on your Mac; the only thing Nomima ever fetches from the web is the exact page you chose to bookmark. Nothing else is sent anywhere. For the full picture, see Settings & privacy.
